Rethinking Critics and Policies for Legged Locomotion: An Empirical Study

Lucky Kant Nayak, Aryan Satpathy, Shohei Nagai
Carnegie Mellon University

Abstract

We study the impact of reinforcement learning paradigms, critic design, and policy architecture on simulated quadruped locomotion. Using Legged Gym, we train Go2 velocity-tracking policies with PPO, SAC, and MBPO, representing on-policy, off-policy, and model-based RL, respectively. We also evaluate two PPO-based modifications: a multi-critic value function that decomposes rewards by category, and a Transformer-based policy that models histories of proprioceptive observations. PPO provides the strongest overall baseline, achieving a final mean reward of 23.67 and a velocity tracking success rate of 95.16%. SAC learns a reasonable but weaker policy, with 21.24 reward and 86.32% success, while MBPO fails to learn stable locomotion, plateauing at 6.42 reward and 2.23% success. The Multi-Critic variant achieves the highest task reward and tracking success rate of 95.46%, but produces unrealistic motions with large regularization costs. The Transformer-based policy reaches competitive performance, with 21.88 reward and 91.94% success, but learns more slowly than PPO. Overall, our results show that on-policy RL remains a strong choice for massively parallel legged locomotion, while critic decomposition and temporal self-attention introduce trade-offs among task performance, motion realism, and optimization difficulty.
