Rethinking Critics and Policies for Legged Locomotion: An Empirical Study

Lucky Kant Nayak, Aryan Satpathy, Shohei Nagai
Carnegie Mellon University

Abstract

We study the impact of architectural and value-function design choices on learning locomotion policies in simulation. Using the Legged Gym framework, we train quadruped control policies with three representative reinforcement learning paradigms: on-policy policy-gradient, off-policy Q-function, and model-based reinforcement learning. We then evaluate two modifications applied consistently across these methods. First, we replace the standard single critic with a multi-critic formulation that decomposes the value function across groups of reward terms. Second, we replace the default recurrent policy architecture with a transformer-based policy to model histories of observations. Through controlled experiments, we analyze how these changes affect learning stability, sample efficiency, and final performance across algorithms. With this project, we aim to provide an empirical comparison that clarifies the role of critic structure and sequence modeling choices in legged-robot reinforcement learning.
